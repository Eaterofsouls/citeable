## 1. Research Methodology

Five parallel research workstreams ran concurrently:

1. **AI Crawler / robots.txt** — Verified all 8 major AI crawler user-agents against official platform documentation.
2. **llms.txt** — Investigated the specification, adoption status, and evidence of measurable effect.
3. **Structured Data / AI Citation** — Verified the contested relationship between JSON-LD schema and AI citation frequency.
4. **Legal / Policy** — Verified current ToS language and the SerpApi vs Google legal situation.
5. **Technical crawling / rendering** — Verified the C200-series technical claims against Google Search Central and IETF standards.

Additionally, internal provenance research was conducted on the C200-C324 series to trace their origin document.

**Source hierarchy applied:** Tier 1 (official platform docs, RFC standards, court documents) > Tier 2 (peer-reviewed / large-scale research) > Tier 3 (reputable practitioners with disclosed methodology) > Tier 4 (practitioner blogs, vendor content).

**Non-negotiable constraints:**
- LLM agreement was not treated as independent evidence.
- Correlation was never promoted to causation.
- Contradictions were preserved rather than resolved artificially.

## 3. AI Crawler Knowledge — Full Verification

### 3.1 CRITICAL FINDING: GPTBOT_MISSING and GOOGLE_EXTENDED_MISSING Are Invalid Check Codes

**This is the most operationally significant finding of the entire research phase.**

The current Citeable check codes conflate two completely separate concepts:
- **AI Model Training Crawlers:** `GPTBot`, `ClaudeBot`, `Google-Extended` — control whether content is used to train future AI models
- **AI Search / Citation Crawlers:** `OAI-SearchBot`, `Claude-SearchBot`, `PerplexityBot`, `Googlebot` — control whether content appears in AI-generated answers

**Official documentation confirms:**
- Blocking `GPTBot` does NOT prevent ChatGPT from citing your site (ChatGPT Search uses `OAI-SearchBot`)
- `Google-Extended` explicitly has **no impact on Google Search or AI Overviews** — Google's own documentation states: *"Google-Extended does not impact a site's inclusion or ranking in Google Search, nor does it control AI Overviews."*
- Blocking `ClaudeBot` does NOT prevent Claude from citing your site (Claude Search uses `Claude-SearchBot`)

**Consequence:** A site that blocks `GPTBot` and `Google-Extended` passes zero of Citeable's GPTBOT_MISSING / GOOGLE_EXTENDED_MISSING checks — yet retains 100% AI search citation eligibility. Conversely, a site that allows only these training crawlers gains zero citation benefit.

### 3.3 C013 Verification: robots.txt Enforceability

**Verification result: Verified with Qualification**

Technically: `robots.txt` is a voluntary protocol (RFC 9309). No server-side enforcement mechanism exists.

Legally: The claim requires **geographic qualification**:
- **US:** Robots.txt violation is not criminal computer fraud/trespass (per CFAA case law: *hiQ v. LinkedIn*, *Meta v. Bright Data*).
- **EU:** Under EU DSM Directive Article 4 (2019/790) and 2025/2026 German case law, a machine-readable `robots.txt` constitutes an enforceable copyright TDM opt-out signal.

**The current C013 statement ("voluntary convention without enforcement") is accurate for US/technical contexts but incomplete globally.**

### 4.2 Platform Adoption (Official Documentation)

| Platform | Officially reads llms.txt? | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google | **Explicitly No** | Google Search Central docs (June 2026) explicitly state Google Search does NOT use llms.txt. John Mueller/Gary Illyes confirmed on record. | https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/generative-ai |
| OpenAI | **No search support** | No documentation indicating GPTBot or ChatGPT Search reads llms.txt for web indexing. | — |
| Anthropic | **No search support** | ClaudeBot does not use llms.txt for general web discovery or citations. | — |
| Perplexity | **No search ranking support** | No llms.txt as retrieval/ranking signal. | — |
| Cursor / Windsurf / Claude Code | **Officially supported** | IDEs and developer tools (Cursor, Windsurf, Claude Code, Aider, LangChain, LlamaIndex, Mintlify, GitBook, Cloudflare) actively support llms.txt for developer context. | — |

**Finding: No major consumer AI search platform officially reads llms.txt for search citation purposes. Google has explicitly stated it does not use it. The file IS supported by developer coding tools, which is a completely different use case.**

### 4.3 Evidence of Measurable Effect

**Unsupported (Refuted by empirical data)**

The research found three independent empirical studies, all showing no effect:

1. **Ahrefs Server Log Study (May 2026, 137,210 domains):** 97.0% of `llms.txt` files received ZERO requests from AI crawlers. The remaining 3% were mostly SEO audit tools/scanners.
2. **SE Ranking 300,000 Domain Study:** Analysis across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini revealed **zero correlation** between llms.txt presence and citation frequency. An XGBoost predictive model improved in accuracy when the llms.txt feature was *removed*.
3. **Search Engine Land Field Study (10 sites):** 8 of 9 analyzed sites saw zero measurable traffic or citation change after implementing llms.txt.

**The MKB Study B claims C016-C018 were correct.** There is a **direct, verified contradiction** between the MKB and the Citeable check code:
- MKB Study B (C016-C018): llms.txt has no measurable effect on AI citation ✓ **Confirmed correct**
- Citeable check codes: `LLMS_TXT_MISSING` deducts −6 points from access score and is treated as an urgent deficiency ✗ **Factually incorrect**

**Specification note:** llms.txt was proposed by Jeremy Howard (Answer.AI) on 2024-09-03. Version 2 released August 2026. Not an IETF/W3C/ISO standard. Origin: https://llmstxt.org

**Verdict:** `LLMS_TXT_MISSING` check premise is **refuted by empirical evidence**. The check should be demoted to an informational developer-readiness recommendation, not an access score deduction. The existing MKB claims C016-C018 are verified and should be the canonical representation.

### 5.3 Entity Disambiguation (CLM-057 — Separate Question)

This is a **different question from citation frequency** and has separate evidence:
- Google's Knowledge Graph uses `@id` and `sameAs` links (to Wikidata, Wikipedia, Crunchbase) for entity reconciliation and Knowledge Panel formation — **documented in Google Knowledge Graph Search API**
- In RAG/vector ingestion pipelines, structured key-value entity definitions reduce semantic polysemy — **documented by inference from RAG engineering literature**
- The Williams-Cook experiment proves LLMs process JSON-LD as raw text tokens, which actually *supports* entity disambiguation (they read the entity information) while not supporting citation frequency uplift

**Verdict for CLM-057:** Supported by reasonable inference and direct Google Knowledge Graph documentation. Not contraindicated by the citation-frequency studies (different question).

## 6. Legal / Policy Claims — Verification

### 6.1 CLM-043: ToS Prohibits Consumer UI Scraping

**Verification Result: Verified**

Precise ToS language verified (effective dates as of Aug 2026):

- **OpenAI ToS (§ 2(c), effective Jan 1 2026):** *"You may not... use any automated or programmatic method to extract data or output from the Services, including scraping, web harvesting, or web data extraction..."*
- **Google ToS (effective Jul 30 2026):** Users may not *"send automated queries of any sort to Google's systems without express prior permission."*
- **Perplexity ToS:** Users agree not to *"use any robot, spider, crawler, scraper, or other automated means or interface not provided by us to access the Services or to extract data"*

**New finding:** All four frontier AI platforms provide official APIs with purpose-built citation data structures:
- **Google Gemini API:** `groundingMetadata` → `groundingChunks` (source URLs/titles) + `groundingSupports` (text-span to source mappings) — purpose-built for programmatic citation attribution
- **Perplexity Sonar API:** Top-level `citations` array + `search_results` metadata
- **OpenAI Responses API:** `web_search` tool returns source citations
- **Anthropic Claude API:** `web_search` tool returns citations with source links

### 6.2 CLM-044: Google DMCA Case Against SerpApi — Updated with Precise Ruling

**Verification Result: Verified with Qualification**

Case: *Google LLC v. SerpApi, LLC*, No. 4:25-cv-10826-YGR (N.D. Cal.), Chief Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers, July 20, 2026.

**Precise breakdown of the ruling:**
- **Public SERPs (URLs, snippets, rankings):** Dismissed **WITH PREJUDICE** — Standard search results are non-copyrightable factual data. DMCA anti-circumvention statute cannot protect access to non-copyrightable works.
- **Licensed third-party content (Knowledge Panels, licensed images):** Dismissed **with leave to amend** — Google hadn't sufficiently pled authorization from underlying copyright holders.

**August 10, 2026 update:** Google filed First Amended Complaint incorporating third-party licensing agreements (Reddit, etc.). Case Management Conference set Oct 7, 2026.

**Practical consequence:** The "SearchGuard = DRM" theory failed for public SERP data, with prejudice. Google's contract/ToS breach claims remain a separate, active issue.

### 6.3 CLM-045: Gemini API Terms and Citation Tracking

**Verification Result: Verified**

Gemini API Additional Terms (last modified March 23, 2026): prohibit competing model training and parameter weight extraction. Contain **no restriction** against citation tracking, brand monitoring, or reasonable-volume automated queries. The API actively provides `groundingMetadata` designed for programmatic citation consumption — the use case is explicitly supported.

### 6.4 CLM-046: Vendor Best Practices

**Verification Result: Verified**

All major GEO/AEO vendors explicitly advise: no DIY browser scraping of consumer UIs; use official APIs; implement exponential backoff; cache AI search responses 12-24 hours.

## 17. Final Summary

### Of the ~88 current knowledge concepts, how many are:
- **Well supported:** ~35 (T1-sourced C200 series technical facts, crawler identity/purpose documentation, robots.txt RFC, nosnippet for AI Overviews, JSON-LD validity for rich results, ToS language, citation measurement as sampling)
- **Supported with qualifications:** ~18 (entity disambiguation via schema, content extractability inference, passage retrieval, crawl budget as operational concept, page rank → AI citation correlation)
- **Contested:** ~10 (schema → citation frequency, PerplexityBot compliance, organic rank's causal vs correlational role in AI citation)
- **Outdated / Superseded:** ~3 (CLM-044 DMCA theory; GPTBOT_MISSING as citation signal; GOOGLE_EXTENDED_MISSING as citation signal)
- **Unsupported / Refuted:** ~8 (vendor citation statistics, llms.txt as citation readiness signal, page speed as AI citation factor, core web vitals as AI ranking factor, schema schema-is-neutral-to-AI-Overviews claim from vendors)
- **Historical/process:** ~11 (M001-M006, outcome logs, tool landscape pricing)
- **Requiring more research:** ~3 (Applebot-Extended, CCBot relevance, content freshness signaling)

### How many genuinely new knowledge concepts were discovered?
**10 new knowledge concepts:**
- GAP-01 through GAP-08 from the Phase 3 gap map
- **GAP-09:** Gemini API `groundingMetadata` is purpose-built for citation tracking — the use case is affirmatively supported, not merely not-prohibited
- **GAP-10:** Core Web Vitals / page speed are NOT documented AI citation signals (refutes an implicit assumption in current audit scope)

### How many existing concepts had their provenance recovered?
**~20 claims** had their canonical source URLs improved or recovered, including: OpenAI bot docs URL migration resolved, Ahrefs schema study confirmed with precise methodology, Fischman SSRN confirmed with COI noted, Anthropic support URL corrected, PerplexityBot controversy documented via Wired + Cloudflare telemetry, Google-Extended explicit statement recovered, Google ToS effective date confirmed, precise SerpApi case number confirmed, Gemini API Terms modification date confirmed, llms.txt origin attributed to Jeremy Howard / Answer.AI with v2 date.

### Which knowledge should absolutely NOT enter the canonical runtime system?
1. Any check or claim framing `GPTBOT_MISSING` or `GOOGLE_EXTENDED_MISSING` as AI search citation readiness signals — **directly contradicted by official documentation**
2. `llms.txt` presented as a verified, officially-supported search citation signal — **refuted by three empirical studies and explicit Google denial**
3. Vendor citation statistics with no methodology (BrightEdge "44%", SE Ranking "71%", Stackmatix "2.5x", etc.) — **no sample, no control group, COI**
4. `PRIORITY_SCORES` (1–13) presented as empirical rankings — **heuristic business policy only**
5. The DMCA-as-enforcement-teeth framing for CLM-044 — **dismissed with prejudice**

### Which knowledge is safe to make canonical?
KT-01 through KT-19 (with noted qualifications). The C200 T1-sourced claims (55 of 96). CLM-019–CLM-031 crawler claims (updated with the training/search purpose distinction).

### Which knowledge needs human judgment?
- **PerplexityBot compliance:** Officially claims compliance; empirically observed to bypass. Legal and product risk assessment required before publishing advice.
- **llms.txt for developer tools:** Legitimate recommendation for sites targeting developer-tool audiences (Cursor, Claude Code); harmful advice for sites targeting search citation improvement.
- **Schema for entity disambiguation:** Directionally correct inference but not quantity-verified. Safe to recommend, but "may help" framing required.
- **Schema → AI citation (contested):** The null-to-negative result is the best current evidence but the field is active. A new large-scale study could shift this.

### Which knowledge remains unknown?
- The exact algorithmic signals Google, OpenAI, Perplexity, and Anthropic use for citation selection (deliberately undisclosed by all platforms)
- Whether Applebot-Extended has any impact on Apple Intelligence answer generation
- Whether content freshness (HTTP Last-Modified, Sitemap `<lastmod>`) affects AI crawl recency vs. citation probability
- The effect of passage-level content clarity on AI-specific extraction vs. on general search ranking

### Which AEOG phases now have sufficient knowledge coverage?
- **AP-02 (Schema/JSON-LD):** Good — contested areas now properly characterized
- **AP-03 (Extractability/Crawlers):** Good — after correcting training/search conflation and demoting llms.txt
- **AP-08 (Legal/Policy):** Good — all four claims verified; DMCA qualification added

### Which phases still have material gaps?
- **AP-04 (Authority):** Only proxy evidence (organic rank → AI citation); no direct AI-specific authority signal documentation
- **AP-05 (Citation Measurement):** Well-described methodology but no ground-truth access; repeated sampling fragile
- **AP-06 (Competitive Intelligence):** Only vendor tool landscape data; no verified methodology for competitive gap analysis
- **AP-10 (Remediation Planning):** No knowledge backing; only heuristic `PRIORITY_SCORES`

### Final estimated size of the useful knowledge corpus:
- **Conservative:** ~44 well-evidenced, directly actionable concepts (KT-01 to KT-19 + 25 T1-sourced C200 claims)
- **Likely:** ~67 (including qualified/partially-supported knowledge with appropriate uncertainty labels)
- **Upper bound:** ~82 (if all contested knowledge is included with explicit uncertainty markers)

**The useful runtime corpus that Citeable can act on with reasonable confidence: ~44 concepts.**

> **The most important single outcome of this phase:** The research revealed that two of Citeable's current check codes (`GPTBOT_MISSING`, `GOOGLE_EXTENDED_MISSING`) actively mislead users — they present AI training crawler allowance as a citation readiness metric, which official platform documentation explicitly contradicts. Fixing this is the highest-value single action before any architectural work begins.
